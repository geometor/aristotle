Physics II.2 — Structural Synopsis
==================================

.. rubric:: Thematic Synopsis & Argument Progression · Bekker 193b22–194b15

Architectural Thesis
--------------------

In *Physics* II.2 (`193b22–194b15`) and III.4–7 (`202b30–208a4`), :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` demarcates the exact ontological boundary between physics and geometry—showing how the mathematician legitimately :term:`separates <chorismos>` surfaces, volumes, lengths, and points in thought from sensible motion without incurring falsity (`193b31–35`)—and constructs his revolutionary theory of the :term:`potential infinite <apeiron>` (*dynamei apeiron*), proving that geometers require no actual infinite line (`207b27–34`).

Logical & Noetic Progression
----------------------------

**Physics II.2 (193b22–194b15) — Natural Philosopher vs. Mathematician: Curve vs. Snub**

Physical bodies contain surfaces, volumes, lines, and points (`193b24`), yet the mathematician treats them *not qua boundaries of natural mobile bodies*. Contrasts 'the curved' (*to kampylon*, defined without sensible matter) with 'the snub' (*to simon*, concavity essentially bound up with nose-flesh), while noting that optics, harmonics, and astronomy are 'the more physical branches of mathematics' (`τὰ φυσικώτερα τῶν μαθημάτων`, `194a7`).
