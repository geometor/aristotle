Physics III.7 — Structural Synopsis
===================================

.. rubric:: Thematic Synopsis & Argument Progression · Bekker 207a33–208a4

Architectural Thesis
--------------------

In *Physics* II.2 (`193b22–194b15`) and III.4–7 (`202b30–208a4`), :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` demarcates the exact ontological boundary between physics and geometry—showing how the mathematician legitimately :term:`separates <chorismos>` surfaces, volumes, lengths, and points in thought from sensible motion without incurring falsity (`193b31–35`)—and constructs his revolutionary theory of the :term:`potential infinite <apeiron>` (*dynamei apeiron*), proving that geometers require no actual infinite line (`207b27–34`).

Logical & Noetic Progression
----------------------------

**Physics III.7 (207a33–208a4) — Inverse Asymmetry of Number & Magnitude; Sufficiency for Geometry**

Establishes the exact duality: number has a minimum (the indivisible :term:`monad <monas>`) and is potentially infinite by *addition*, whereas continuous :term:`magnitude <megethos>` has a maximum (bounded by the finite cosmos) and is potentially infinite by *division* (`207b1–15`). Concludes (`207b27–34`) that this deprives mathematicians of nothing: they need only a finite straight line as long as they please (`ὅσην ἂν βούλωνται πεπερασμένην`), since any given ratio in the largest magnitude can be cut identically in the smallest!
