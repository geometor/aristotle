===================================
Physics III.6 — Structural Synopsis
===================================

.. rubric:: Thematic Synopsis & Argument Progression · Bekker 206a9–207a32

Architectural Thesis
--------------------

In *Physics* II.2 (`193b22–194b15`) and III.4–7 (`202b30–208a4`), :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` demarcates the exact ontological boundary between physics and geometry—showing how the mathematician legitimately :term:`separates <chorismos>` surfaces, volumes, lengths, and points in thought from sensible motion without incurring falsity (`193b31–35`)—and constructs his revolutionary theory of the :term:`potential infinite <apeiron>` (*dynamei apeiron*), proving that geometers require no actual infinite line (`207b27–34`).

Logical & Noetic Progression
----------------------------

**Physics III.6 (206a9–207a32) — Potential Existence of the Infinite (Dynamei Apeiron)**

Saves both continuous division and number without an actual infinite: the infinite exists *potentially*, like a day or the Olympic games whose being consists in one thing always coming into existence after another (`206a21–25`). Reverses the vulgar definition: the infinite is *not* 'that outside which nothing is', but **'that outside which there is always something'** (`οὗ ἀεί τι ἔξω ἐστίν`, `206b33–207a2`).
