==================================================
Aristotle's Four Pillars of Geometric Cognition
==================================================

.. rubric:: Synthesizing Mathematical History (Heath) and Scholastic Noetics (Hoenen)

*Loci Geometrici et Noetici* organizes fifty-four Aristotelian chapters into five *Libri* corresponding to the four structural pillars that sustain classical constructive geometry.

I. Apodeixis & First Principles (*Organon*)
-------------------------------------------

In *Posterior Analytics* I.1–13 (:doc:`Liber I.A </loci_geometrici/liber-1a-apodeixis/liber-1a-apodeixis-en>`), :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` demonstrates that scientific knowledge (:term:`epistēmē <episteme>`) cannot regress infinitely nor move in a circle. Every demonstrative science rests upon three distinct kinds of indemonstrable starting-points (``72a14–24``, ``76a31–77a4``):

1. **Common Axioms** (:term:`axiōmata <axioma>`): Principles presupposed by all reasoning, such as "if equals be taken from equals, the remainders are equal"—adopted by :person:`Euclid <euclid>` as the *Common Notions*.
2. **Nominal Definitions**: Understanding *what* a term signifies (e.g., unit, straight, triangle), without yet assuming existence.
3. **Existential Hypotheses & Constructive Postulates** (:term:`aitēmata <aitema>`): Positing the primary subjects (points and lines) and licensing elementary compass-and-straightedge constructions from which all composite figures are proved to exist without contradiction.

Equally decisive is Aristotle's requirement of **commensurate universality** (:term:`katholou <katholou>`, *An. Post.* I.4–5, ``73b25–74b4``): proving ``2R`` separately for equilateral, isosceles, and scalene triangles fails to capture the true middle term, whereas :person:`Eudoxus's <eudoxus>` general theory of proportion (:cite:`Elements V, Def. 5 <euclid:V.def.5>`) grasps alternation at its exact level of intelligible generality.

II. Continuous Magnitude & Potential Infinity (*Physica* & *De Caelo*)
----------------------------------------------------------------------

In *Categories* 6 (``4b20``, :doc:`Liber II.A </loci_geometrici/liber-2a-quantitas-et-syllogismus/liber-2a-quantitas-et-syllogismus-en>`) and *Physics* III–VI (:doc:`Liber III.A </loci_geometrici/liber-3a-mathematicus-et-infinitum/liber-3a-mathematicus-et-infinitum-en>` & :doc:`Liber III.B </loci_geometrici/liber-3b-topos-continuum-dimensio/liber-3b-topos-continuum-dimensio-en>`), Aristotle severs **discrete multitude** (:term:`arithmos <arithmos>`) from **continuous magnitude** (:term:`megethos <megethos>`). Two quantities are :term:`continuous <syneches>` when their extremities are *one*; therefore no line can ever be composed of unextended points (:term:`stigmai <stigme>`, *Phys.* VI.1, ``231a21–b18``).

Simultaneously, *Physics* III.6–7 (``206a9–208a4``) resolves the antinomies of :person:`Zeno of Elea <zeno>` and guarantees Euclidean constructibility: the infinite (:term:`apeiron <apeiron>`) exists **potentially by division**, never as a completed infinite body, assuring geometers that any finite segment can be bisected or cut in extreme and mean ratio indefinitely.

III. Common Sensibles & the Spatial Phantasm (*De Anima* III)
-------------------------------------------------------------

How do non-empirical geometric necessities enter human thought? In *De Anima* III.3–8 (:doc:`Liber IV </loci_geometrici/liber-4-de-anima-phantasia/liber-4-de-anima-phantasia-en>`), Aristotle shows that magnitude, figure, number, movement, and rest are **common sensibles** (:term:`aisthēta koina <aistheta-koina>`, ``418a17``, ``428b22``) perceived across sight and touch and preserved by imagination (:term:`phantasia <phantasia>`) as spatial **phantasms** (:term:`phantasmata <phantasma>`). Crucially, "the soul never thinks without a phantasm" (``431a16``), and "the faculty of thinking thinks the forms in the phantasms" (*τὰ μὲν οὖν εἴδη τὸ νοητικὸν ἐν τοῖς φαντάσμασι νοεῖ*, ``431b2``)—even when drawing a determinate foot-long segment while reasoning solely about its universal linearity (*De Memoria* ``449b30``, *An. Post.* I.10 ``76b39``).

IV. Intelligible Matter & Diagrammatic Actuality (*Metaphysica* Z, Θ, M)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Finally, *Metaphysics* Z.10–11, Θ.9, and M.1–3 (:doc:`Liber V.B </loci_geometrici/liber-5b-hyle-noete-energeia-pulchritudo/liber-5b-hyle-noete-energeia-pulchritudo-en>`) supply the ontological counterpart to *De Anima*:

* **Intelligible Matter** (:term:`hylē noētē <hyle-noete>`, ``1036a9–12``): When abstraction (:term:`aphairesis <aphairesis>`) strips away sensible qualities (heat, weight, color), pure continuous extension remains as the individuating substrate enabling two congruent circles or triangles to be numerically distinct while sharing one definition.
* **Diagrammatic Discovery in Act** (:term:`energeia <energeia>`, ``1051a21–33``): Latent geometric truths—such as the interior angles of a triangle equaling two right angles (:cite:`Elements I.32 <euclid:I.32>`) or the angle in a semicircle being universally right (:cite:`Elements III.31 <euclid:III.31>`)—become immediately transparent to intellectual vision (:term:`noēsis <nous>`) the instant the geometer actualizes the latent division by drawing the auxiliary parallel or radius.
