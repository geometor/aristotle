=======================================
On the Soul III.7 — Structural Synopsis
=======================================

.. rubric:: Thematic Synopsis & Argument Progression · Bekker 431a1–431b19

Architectural Thesis
--------------------

In *De Anima* III.3–8 (`427a17–432a14`), :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` unveils the cognitive psychology that makes geometry possible for an embodied intellect: distinguishing :term:`imagination <phantasia>` from both sensation and judgment, distinguishing receptive and active :term:`intellect <nous>`, explaining how the mind thinks undivided continuous lengths and indivisible boundaries (`430b6–20`), and establishing the canonical law that **the soul never thinks without a phantasm** (`431a16`, `432a8`) and thinks mathematicals (:term:`ta ex aphaireseōs <aphairesis>`, `431b12–17`) within imaginative forms.

Logical & Noetic Progression
----------------------------

**De Anima III.7–8 (431a1–432a14) — Noesis in Phantasmata & Objects of Abstraction (431b12–17)**

Declares that to the thinking soul :term:`phantasmata <phantasma>` serve as percepts (`431a14–17`), that the faculty of knowledge thinks the forms in the images (`τὰ μὲν οὖν εἴδη τὸ νοητικὸν ἐν τοῖς φαντάσμασι νοεῖ`, `431b2`), and that the mind thinks **mathematical objects of abstraction** (`τὰ ἐν ἀφαιρέσει λεγόμενα`, `431b12`) just as one might think concavity in isolation from the nose-flesh in which it occurs (`431b12–17`). Concludes in III.8 that intellect is the 'form of forms' (`εἶδος εἰδῶν`, `432a2`) and that without perception and phantasmata nobody could ever learn or understand anything (`432a7–9`).
