=======================================
On the Soul III.6 — Structural Synopsis
=======================================

.. rubric:: Thematic Synopsis & Argument Progression · Bekker 430a26–431a1

Architectural Thesis
--------------------

In *De Anima* III.3–8 (`427a17–432a14`), :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` unveils the cognitive psychology that makes geometry possible for an embodied intellect: distinguishing :term:`imagination <phantasia>` from both sensation and judgment, distinguishing receptive and active :term:`intellect <nous>`, explaining how the mind thinks undivided continuous lengths and indivisible boundaries (`430b6–20`), and establishing the canonical law that **the soul never thinks without a phantasm** (`431a16`, `432a8`) and thinks mathematicals (:term:`ta ex aphaireseōs <aphairesis>`, `431b12–17`) within imaginative forms.

Logical & Noetic Progression
----------------------------

**De Anima III.6 (430a26–431a1) — Thinking of Indivisibles, Undivided Length & Point**

Explains how the mind thinks an actually undivided line segment (`μῆκος ἀδιαίρετον ἐνεργείᾳ`) in an undivided time, whereas a :term:`point <stigme>` and every division are made known like privation (`ὥσπερ ἡ στέρησις`, `430b20–21`).
