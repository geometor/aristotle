Liber II.B — Intelligible Priority in Definitions & Antanairesis — Structural Synopsis¶

Argumentative Architecture & Bekker Conspectus (Bekker 141a23–142b19, 158a31–159a14)

Architectural Thesis¶

In Topics VI.4 (141a23–142b19) and VIII.3 (158a31–159a14), Aristotle establishes the canon of scientific definition—insisting that geometric entities must be defined through what is prior and more intelligible by nature rather than through sensible posterior effects—and preserves the priceless pre-Eudoxian definition of geometric proportion through antanairesis (reciprocal subtraction, 158b29–35).

Logical & Noetic Progression¶

Topics VI.4 (141a23–142b19) — Intelligible Priority in Defining Point, Line, Plane & Solid

Distinguishes what is better known ‘to us’ (hēmin, sensible solid bodies) from what is better known ‘unqualifiedly / by nature’ (haplōs, point -> line -> plane -> solid). Critiques definitions that invert natural priority by defining point as ‘limit of a line’ or line as ‘limit of a plane’ (141b19–22), as well as circular definitions.

Topics VIII.3 (158a31–159a14) — Primary Hypotheses & Proportion via Antanairesis (158b29–35)

Explains why certain arguments are difficult to attack or establish because they stand near primary hypotheses, illustrating how in mathematics even proving that Parallelograms/Triangles of the same height are proportional to their bases (Elements VI.1) was once arduous before the clear definition of ‘having the same ratio’ via antanairesis (τὴν αὐτὴν ἀνταναίρεσιν ἔχει τὰ χωρία καὶ αἱ βάσεις, 158b33–34).
