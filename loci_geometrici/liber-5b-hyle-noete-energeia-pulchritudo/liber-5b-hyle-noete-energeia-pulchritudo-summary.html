Liber V.B — Intelligible Matter, Constructive Actuality & Mathematical Beauty — Structural Synopsis¶

Argumentative Architecture & Bekker Conspectus (Bekker 1034b–1037a, 1051a, 1076a–1078b)

Architectural Thesis¶

Liber V.B crowns the entire Loci Geometrici et Noetici Codex with the three supreme peaks of Aristotelian philosophy of mathematics: Metaphysics Z.10–11 (1034b20–1037a20) introducing intelligible matter (hylē noētē, 1036a9–12), Metaphysics $\Theta$.9 (1051a4–33) revealing that geometrical theorems are discovered by actualizing (energeia, 1051a21–33) latent divisions in construction, and Metaphysics M.1–3 (1076a8–1078b6) articulating the qua-ontology of mathematicals and celebrating mathematical beauty (to kalon: order, symmetry, and definiteness, 1078a31–b6).

Logical & Noetic Progression¶

Metaphysics Z (VII).10–11 (1034b20–1037a20) — Formal Parts vs. Intelligible Matter (1036a9)

Solves why ‘semicircle’ is not part of the definition of ‘circle’, whereas ‘right angle’ is presupposed in the definition of ‘acute angle’ (1035b4–12): semicircles are parts of an individual circle’s intelligible matter (ὕλη δὲ ἡ μὲν αἰσθητή ἐστιν ἡ δὲ νοητή, αἰσθητὴ μὲν οἷον χαλκὸς καὶ ξύλον… νοητὴ δὲ ἡ ἐν τοῖς αἰσθητοῖς ὑπάρχουσα μὴ ᾗ αἰσθητά, οἷον τὰ μαθηματικά, 1036a9–12). Even when all bronze and stone are abstracted, an individual mathematical circle still possesses pure spatial extension individuating its congruent parts (1036b32–1037a5).

Metaphysics Θ (IX).9 (1051a4–1051a33) — Constructive Actuality (Energeia) in Geometrical Discovery

Demonstrates the cognitive priority of actuality over potency (1051a21–33): ‘Geometrical constructions (`τὰ διαγράμματα`) are discovered by an activity (`ἐνεργείᾳ`); for it is by dividing (`διαιροῦντες`) that they discover them.’ Gives two immortal Euclidean proofs: (1) why a triangle’s interior angles equal 2R—the moment the parallel to the base is drawn (ἀνῆκτο ἡ παρὰ τὴν πλευράν), it is immediately clear on seeing it (ἰδόντι ἂν ἦν εὐθὺς δῆλον, 1051a24–26); and (2) why the angle in a semicircle is universally a right angle (Elements III.31)—by erecting the third equal radius from the midpoint of the diameter (1051a26–29).

Metaphysics M (XIII).1–2 (1076a8–1077b16) — Critique of Immanent & Separate Mathematical Solids

Refutes both the view that mathematical solids exist as separate entities inside sensible bodies (which would place two solids in the same place and make sensible bodies indivisible) and the Platonic view that they exist apart from sensible bodies (which generates an infinite cascade of duplicate astronomical spheres and elevates what is prior only in formula [τῷ λόγῳ] over what is prior in substance [τῇ οὐσίᾳ, 1077a36–b11]).

Metaphysics M (XIII).3 (1077b17–1078b6) — Qua-Predication & Mathematical Beauty (1078a31–b6)

Establishes Aristotle’s positive qua-theory (ᾗ): just as truths hold of moving things qua moving apart from what they are, geometry studies sensible bodies not qua sensible, but qua lengths and planes (1077b22–34), achieving higher exactitude the simpler its aspect (1078a9–17). Closes with the sublime defence of mathematical beauty (1078a31–b6): whereas the good concerns action, the beautiful (`τὸ καλόν`) exists also in motionless things, and its supreme forms are order (`τάξις`), symmetry (`συμμετρία`), and definiteness (`τὸ ὡρισμένον`), which the mathematical sciences pre-eminently demonstrate!
