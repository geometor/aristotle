Liber I.B — Universal Demonstration, Exactitude & Intuitive Nous — Critical Commentary & Notes¶

Historical-Mathematical (Heath) & Scholastic-Noetic (Hoenen) Apparatus · Bekker 85a13–88a17, 99b15–100b17

I. Mathematical-Historical Commentary (Secundum Heath & Euclidem)¶

Heath (Thirteen Books, Vol. I, p. 322; Mathematics in Aristotle, pp. 61–68) highlights 85b37–86a3 as our earliest explicit Greek attestation of the general theorem extending Elements I.32: that the exterior angles of any rectilinear figure (triangle, quadrilateral, etc.), when the sides are produced in order, are together equal to four right angles (4R). Proclus later preserved this corollary in his commentary on Euclid I.32.

In 87a35–37, Aristotle records the Pythagorean-Academic definition of the arithmetical unit as ‘substance without position’ (οὐσία ἄθετος) and the geometrical point as ‘substance having position’ (οὐσία θετός). As Heath notes (Vol. I, p. 155), Euclid deliberately replaced this ontological definition in Elements I, Def. 1 (Σημεῖόν ἐστιν, οὗ μέρος οὐθέν — ‘A point is that which has no part’) to keep geometry independent of Pythagorean monadic ontology while retaining indivisibility.

II. Scholastic-Noetic Commentary (Secundum Hoenen, Thomam & Paciolum)¶

For Hoenen (De Noetica Geometriae, Caput III, n. 1, p. 71; Caput V, pp. 159–189), Posterior Analytics I.31 (87b35–39) and II.19 (99b15–100b17) form a single indivisible arch. In Caput III, Hoenen deploys 87b35—‘even if it were possible to perceive by sense that the triangle has its angles equal to two right angles, we should still seek a demonstration’—to demolish John Stuart Mill’s and Hermann von Helmholtz’s geometric empiricism. Sensory measurement always halts before a threshold of exactitude (limen exactitudinis); it can at best report approximate physical coincidence here and now, never strict equality (= 2R) or universal necessity.

Conversely, in Caput V, Hoenen reads Posterior Analytics II.19 alongside Aquinas (In Anal. Post. II, lect. 20) to show that epagōgē is the exact opposite of Humean enumerative induction. Because mathematical form exists individuated in sensible and imaginative extension, a single clear imaginative presentation suffices—once sensible irrelevancies are abstracted—for nous to apprehend the universal nexus immediately and infallibly.

III. Locus-by-Locus Philological & Geometrical Notes¶

85b4–15 — Isosceles vs. Triangle Qua Triangle

Aristotle contrasts knowing 2R of bronze isosceles triangles with knowing it of triangle qua triangle: removing ‘bronze’ and ‘isosceles’ leaves 2R intact, whereas removing ‘figure’ or ‘limit’ destroys it—exhibiting the exact method of isolating the commensurate subject.

87a35–37 — Ek Prostheseōs (‘By Addition’)

Geometry arises ‘by addition’ to arithmetic not by adding empirical noise, but by adding spatial position (thesis) and continuous dimensionality to indivisibility.

87b35–88a5 — Sense-Perception vs. Universal Demonstration on the Moon & Triangle

Even standing on the moon and watching the earth interpose itself (87b39) would reveal only an individual eclipse occurring now, until intellect grasps the universal middle term from the perceived instance.

100a12–100b17 — The Simile of the Routed Army & Nous as Archē Archēs

Just as one soldier halting in a rout brings a second and then the whole phalanx back to order, one retained percept stabilizes the universal concept in the soul until nous grasps the immediate principle.
