Liber I.B — Universal Demonstration, Exactitude & Intuitive Nous — Structural Synopsis¶

Argumentative Architecture & Bekker Conspectus (Bekker 85a13–88a17, 99b15–100b17)

Architectural Thesis¶

In Analytica Posteriora I.24, I.27, I.31, and II.19 (85a–88a, 99b–100b), Aristotle completes the epistemology of demonstrative science by establishing why commensurate universal proof excels particular proof, why arithmetic exceeds geometry in exactitude, why sensory observation (aisthēsis) can never cross the threshold into scientific necessity (87b35), and how induction from sense-perception culminates in nous as the principle of principles.

Logical & Noetic Progression¶

I.24 (85a13–86a30) — Superiority of Universal over Particular Demonstration

Refutes the illusion that particular proof is ‘more direct’, proving that only universal demonstration exhibits the intrinsic propter quid cause—why the exterior angles of any polygon sum to four right angles (85b38) or why triangle qua triangle possesses 2R (85b5).

I.27 (87a31–87a37) — Exactitude (Akribeia): Monad without Position vs. Point with Position

Ranks theoretical sciences by conceptual simplicity: a science from fewer principles is prior to and more exact than one ‘by addition’ (ek prostheseōs)—as arithmetic (monas = substance without position) is prior to geometry (stigmē = substance having position).

I.31 (87b28–88a17) — Refutation of Geometric Empiricism (87b35)

Proves that sense-perception is necessarily bound to ‘this here and now’ (tode ti, pou, kai nyn). Even if we could directly see on a sensory triangle that its angles equal two right angles, we would still lack epistēmē and seek universal demonstration.

II.19 (99b15–100b17) — Genetic Ascent from Perception to Nous

Resolves how immediate first principles become known without innate Platonic recollection: perception leaves memory, repeated memories form one experience (empeiria), and the universal comes to rest in the soul like a rallying battle-line (100a12), enabling nous to intuit primary principles with certainty exceeding demonstration itself.
