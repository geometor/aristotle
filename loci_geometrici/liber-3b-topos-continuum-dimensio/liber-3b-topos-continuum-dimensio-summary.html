Liber III.B — Place, the Continuum, Zeno’s Paradoxes & Threefold Dimension — Structural Synopsis¶

Argumentative Architecture & Bekker Conspectus (Bekker 208a–213a, 231a–233b, 239b–240a, 268a–b)

Architectural Thesis¶

Liber III.B unites the crowning physical-geometrical analyses of spatial order, continuity, and dimensionality: Physics IV.1–5 (208a27–213a11) defining place as the innermost motionless boundary of the containing body, Physics VI.1–2 & VI.9 (231a21–233b32, 239b5–240a18) proving that no continuum can be composed of indivisible points and resolving Zeno's four paradoxes, and De Caelo I.1 (268a1–b10) demonstrating the completeness of three spatial dimensions.

Logical & Noetic Progression¶

Physics IV.1–3 (208a27–210b31) — Existence & Aporiai of Place (Topos)

Establishes the reality of place from mutual replacement (antimetastasis, 208b1) and natural directional motions, notes that mathematical objects, though lacking physical place, exhibit positional right/left relative to us (208b22–25), refutes Plato's identification of space (chōra) with matter in the Timaeus (209b11–17), and distinguishes eight senses of ‘in’ (210a14–24).

Physics IV.4–5 (210b32–213a11) — Place as Motionless Innermost Boundary (212a20)

Eliminates form, matter, and self-subsistent three-dimensional interval (diastēma), concluding that place is the first motionless boundary of that which contains (τὸ τοῦ περιέχοντος πέρας ἀκίνητον πρῶτον, 212a20–21), solving Zeno’s regress of places (210b22, 212b29).

Physics VI.1–2 (231a21–233b32) — Indivisibility of Points & Isomorphism of Space, Time & Motion

Demonstrates apodictically that no continuum can be made of indivisibles (ἀδύνατον ἐξ ἀδιαιρέτων εἶναί τι συνεχές, οἷον γραμμὴν ἐκ στιγμῶν, 231a24–25), because partless points have neither extremities that can become one nor parts to touch part-to-part, nor can two points even be ‘successive’ (ephexēs) since a divisible line always lies between any two distinct points (231b9). Proves via reciprocal comparison of faster and slower motions (232b20–233a12) that magnitude, time, and motion share identical infinite divisibility.

Physics VI.9 (239b5–240a18) — Refutation of Zeno’s Four Paradoxes of Motion

States and refutes Zeno's Dichotomy (239b11), Achilles (239b14), Flying Arrow (239b5, 239b30—resting on the false premise that time is composed of indivisible ‘nows’), and Moving Rows / Stadium (239b33–240a18).

De Caelo I.1 (268a1–268b10) — Completeness of Three Spatial Dimensions

Defines the continuous as divisible into ever-divisibles and body as divisible in every way (πάντῃ διαιρετόν): magnitude in one dimension is line, in two plane, in three solid body (268a6–8), beyond which there is no transition into another dimension-genus (οὐκ ἔστιν εἰς ἄλλο γένος μετάβασις, 268b1).
