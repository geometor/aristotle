Liber IV — Imagination, Phantasma & Mathematical Abstraction — Critical Commentary & Notes¶

Historical-Mathematical (Heath) & Scholastic-Noetic (Hoenen) Apparatus · Bekker 427a17–432a14

I. Mathematical-Historical Commentary (Secundum Heath & Euclidem)¶

Heath (Thirteen Books, Vol. I, p. 156; Mathematics in Aristotle, pp. 195–199) singles out 430b20–21—‘the point and every division, and whatever is indivisible in this sense, is made known just like privation (`δηλοῦται ὥσπερ ἡ στέρησις`)’—as the deepest explanation of why Euclid opened the Elements with a negative definition of the point (οὗ μέρος οὐθέν, ‘that which has no part’) and defined the line as ‘breadthless length’ (μῆκος ἀπλατές).

Heath also connects De Anima III.4 (429b18–20) directly with Metaphysics Z.10 (1036a9–12): when Aristotle says that ‘the straight’ (to euthy) is like ‘the snub’ because ‘it is bound up with the continuum’ (μετὰ συνεχοῦς γάρ), Aristotle is explicitly identifying the pure spatial continuum with intelligible matter (hylē noētē), judged by a distinct cognitive deployment from sensible flesh!

II. Scholastic-Noetic Commentary (Secundum Hoenen, Thomam & Paciolum)¶

Liber IV is the absolute heartland of Peter Hoenen's De Noetica Geometriae (Caput I, § 2, pp. 14–17; Caput V–VII). Look at the triad of Bekker citations that Hoenen weaves together throughout his treatise: 431a16–17 (οὐδέποτε νοεῖ ἄνευ φαντάσματος ἡ ψυχή), 431b2 (τὰ μὲν οὖν εἴδη τὸ νοητικὸν ἐν τοῖς φαντάσμασι νοεῖ), and 432a8–9 (ὅταν τε θεωρῇ, ἀνάγκη ἅμα φάντασμά τι θεωρεῖν).

Why did modern philosophy split disastrously between Kantian synthetic a priori intuition on one side and Hilbertian/Russellian blind symbolism on the other? Because post-Cartesian rationalism imagined pure intellect operating without imagination, while British empiricism reduced thought to sensory images! Aristotle’s De Anima III.7–8 heals that rupture: human noēsis is neither blind sensory picturing nor disembodied angelism, but abstractive insight reading universal necessity inside the concrete imaginative diagram (conversio ad phantasmata). As Aquinas formulated it in Super Boethium De Trinitate (q. 6, a. 2), natural science terminates at sense, metaphysics at pure intellect, and mathematics terminates at the imagination (ad imaginationem)!

In De Divina Proportione (Compendio Cap. II, fols. 2r–2v), Luca Pacioli invokes De Anima III.8 (432a7–9 — nihil est in intellectu quin prius fuerit in sensu) to defend the primacy of visual geometric perspective among the portals of human wisdom.

III. Locus-by-Locus Philological & Geometrical Notes¶

427b18–20 — Voluntary Geometric Construction Before the Eyes

Unlike involuntary belief, phantasia lies within our constructive power (ἐφ’ ἡμῖν ἐστιν ὅταν βουλώμεθα): the geometer actively projects and transforms figures before the mind’s eye.

429b18–22 — ‘With Continuum’ (Meta Synechous) & Dual Cognitive Faculty

In abstract mathematicals (ἐπὶ τῶν ἐν ἀφαιρέσει ὄντων), ‘the straight’ contains continuous extension (μετὰ συνεχοῦς) as its intelligible substrate, whereas its formal essence (τὸ εὐθεῖ εἶναι) is pure rectilinear determination.

430b6–20 — Actual Undividedness vs. Potential Divisibility of a Segment

When thinking a line segment AB as a single side of a triangle, the intellect thinks it in an undivided act as one whole; only when it bisects AB at C does it think the two halves successively in two half-times!

431b12–17 & 432a3–14 — Phantasmata Without Matter

Phantasmata are like percepts except that they are without physical matter (πλὴν ἄνευ ὕλης, 432a9), yet they are not thoughts (τί διοίσει τὰ πρῶτα νοήματα τῶν φαντασμάτων;, 432a12): thoughts are universal formal contents that cannot exist apart from phantasmata without being reducible to them.
