=======================================
On the Soul III.5 — Structural Synopsis
=======================================

.. rubric:: Thematic Synopsis & Argument Progression · Bekker 430a10–430a25

Architectural Thesis
--------------------

In *De Anima* III.3–8 (`427a17–432a14`), :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>` unveils the cognitive psychology that makes geometry possible for an embodied intellect: distinguishing :term:`imagination <phantasia>` from both sensation and judgment, distinguishing receptive and active :term:`intellect <nous>`, explaining how the mind thinks undivided continuous lengths and indivisible boundaries (`430b6–20`), and establishing the canonical law that **the soul never thinks without a phantasm** (`431a16`, `432a8`) and thinks mathematicals (:term:`ta ex aphaireseōs <aphairesis>`, `431b12–17`) within imaginative forms.

Logical & Noetic Progression
----------------------------

**De Anima III.4–5 (429a10–430a25) — Receptive & Active Intellect; Continuum vs. Essence of Straight**

Shows that intellect must be unmixed to receive all intelligible forms (`429a18`). Crucially distinguishes (`429b10–22`) three tiers: (1) flesh and 'the snub' (sensible matter + form), (2) **'the straight'** (*to euthy*, analogous to the snub because it involves continuous extension, `μετὰ συνεχοῦς γάρ`, `429b19`) vs. 'to be straight' (:term:`to euthei einai <to-ti-en-einai>`), and (3) wholly immaterial essences. Introduces productive intellect (*nous poiētikos*) acting like light (`ὡς ἕξις τις, οἷον τὸ φῶς`, `430a15`).
